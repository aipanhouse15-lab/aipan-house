**Quick answer:** Ghee Sankranti — also called Olgia or Ghee Tyar — is celebrated on Singh (Simha) Sankranti, the first day of the solar month of Bhado. **Ghee Sankranti 2026 falls on Monday, 17 August.** On this day every member of a Kumaoni household eats ghee, ghee is touched to the forehead, and in the older tradition of Olgia, seasonal first-produce and hand-made implements were presented as gifts.

Of all the festivals of the Kumaoni year, Ghee Sankranti has the simplest and most memorable rule: on this one day, you must eat ghee. Not may — must. The saying attached to the rule is repeated to every child in the hills, usually with a grin: whoever does not eat ghee on Ghee Sankranti will be born in the next life as a *ghonga* — a snail. Nobody I have asked believes it literally. Everybody eats the ghee anyway.

## What Ghee Sankranti Is

Ghee Sankranti marks the sun's entry into Simha (Leo) — Singh Sankranti — which opens the solar month of Bhado. In the agricultural rhythm of Kumaon this is the exact middle of the monsoon season. The kharif crops that went into the ground around [Harela](/festivals/harela) in July are now standing in the fields; the paddy has been transplanted; the livestock have had a month of the richest green fodder of the year.

That last point is the heart of the festival. A month of monsoon grass means the buffaloes and cows are giving their best milk of the year, and the best milk makes the best ghee. Ghee Sankranti is the day the household formally tastes that abundance — a festival of dairy at the peak of dairy, in the same way Harela is a festival of seed at the moment of sowing.

Like Harela, it is observed as a folk festival across the Kumaon hills — Almora, Bageshwar, Pithoragarh, Champawat, Nainital — and travels with Kumaoni families wherever they live.

## The Rule About Ghee

The observance is direct. Ghee — ideally fresh, home-made from the season's milk — is eaten by every member of the family, from the eldest to the newest. In many households a small amount of ghee is first touched to the forehead, and to the crown of a child's head, before the meal. Some families extend this to the knees and elbows of infants, a blessing for strong joints and good growth.

The forehead gesture will be familiar to anyone who has read about the [Harela sprout blessing](/festivals/harela) — Kumaoni festivals repeatedly place the substance of the season on the body itself. In July it is green sprouts; in August it is ghee.

## What Is Eaten

The classic Ghee Sankranti plate is built around two things: ghee, and the first tender produce of the monsoon.

The rotis of the day are special — in many homes, *bedu roti*: wheat rotis stuffed with a paste of cooked urad dal, cooked on the tawa and then smeared generously with ghee. Alongside them, the dish most associated with the day is *gaaba* — the tender inner leaves and stalks of the pinalu (taro / arbi) plant, which are exactly at their best in mid-August. The gaaba may be cooked as a vegetable or dipped in batter; either way it represents the first eating of the new season's growth.

Walnuts, cucumbers from the monsoon vines, and whatever else the season has ripened first round out the day. It is a harvest-preview meal, eaten while the main harvest is still weeks away.

## Olgia: the Gift-Giving

The festival's other name, **Olgia**, comes from *olag* — a tradition of presenting seasonal gifts. In the old agrarian arrangement of the hills, this was the day artisans, tenants and workers presented the first produce of the season and objects of their own craft to landowners, patrons and to the local temple: gaaba and cucumbers from the field, ghee and curd from the byre, and hand-made implements — axes, sickles, wooden vessels — from the smiths and carpenters. In return came grain, cloth or money.

The formal patron-client version of Olgia has largely dissolved along with the arrangement that produced it. What survives is the instinct underneath it: Ghee Sankranti remains a day for sending the season's first and best to others — a box of walnuts to a daughter's household, ghee to a neighbour, the first gaaba to whoever in the family loves it most.

## Why Mid-Monsoon

Kumaoni festivals sit at turning points of the agricultural year, and Ghee Sankranti's turning point is easy to miss because nothing visibly begins or ends. What it marks is a checkpoint. By mid-Bhado the sowing gamble taken at Harela has either worked or it has not: the rains have come or failed, the transplanted paddy has taken or rotted. A household that can eat its own ghee and its own first vegetables in the middle of August is a household whose year is on track.

That is why the festival's mood is quieter than Harela's — less ceremony, more table. It is a festival of confirmation. The Aipan drawn for it, where families draw it, is correspondingly simple: a small chowki or threshold marking in the puja room, of the kind documented in [our guide to Aipan's ritual significance](/aipan/aipan-ritual-significance).

The next festival of the Kumaoni year is [Nanda Ashtami](/festivals/nanda-ashtami) in September, and with it [the Nanda Devi melas of Almora and Nainital](/festivals/nanda-devi-mela) — the largest public gatherings of the Kumaoni calendar.

## FAQ

### When is Ghee Sankranti in 2026?

Ghee Sankranti 2026 falls on Monday, August 17 — the day of Singh (Simha) Sankranti, when the sun enters Leo and the solar month of Bhado begins in the Kumaoni calendar.

### Why is ghee eaten on Ghee Sankranti?

Mid-August is the peak of the monsoon fodder season, when cattle give their richest milk of the year, so the ghee is at its best. Folk belief adds a playful warning: whoever skips the ghee will be reborn as a ghonga (snail). The festival formally tastes the season's dairy abundance.

### What is Olgia?

Olgia is the older name of the festival, from "olag" — the presenting of seasonal gifts. Artisans and tenants traditionally presented first produce, ghee, and hand-made implements to patrons and temples on this day. The gift-giving spirit survives in families sending the season's first produce to relatives and neighbours.

### What food is made on Ghee Sankranti?

Bedu roti (urad-stuffed wheat rotis) smeared with ghee, and gaaba — the tender leaves and stalks of the pinalu (arbi/taro) plant, the first vegetable of the monsoon. Walnuts and monsoon cucumbers are also part of the day's eating.

### Which states celebrate Ghee Sankranti?

As Ghee Sankranti or Olgia it is a festival of Uttarakhand, celebrated most strongly in the Kumaon region. The same Singh Sankranti day is observed under other names elsewhere — notably Simha Sankranti in coastal Karnataka.
