Phool Dei is the spring threshold festival of Kumaon, and it belongs to the children. As the first flowers of the season open across the hills, young girls go door to door scattering blossoms, rice and jaggery on each home's threshold — a blessing for the household's prosperity.

## A children's festival

It is one of the few festivals led entirely by the young. Children move from house to house through the village, and at each door they sing *"Phool Dei, chhamma Dei"* — receiving sweets, jaggery, and small gifts in return.

<Callout>**The greeting** — *Phool Dei, chhamma Dei, jaithi dei, dwar bhar bhakar* — a wish that the household's grain stores stay full through the year.</Callout>

## The threshold

The doorstep — the *dehli* — is where Kumaoni life meets the world, and the place Aipan itself is most often drawn. Phool Dei honours that same threshold with flowers instead of rice paste, marking the turn from winter into spring.

## What is made

Households prepare special sweet dishes and rice-flour preparations for the visiting children, and many homes are freshly decorated with Aipan to welcome the season.
