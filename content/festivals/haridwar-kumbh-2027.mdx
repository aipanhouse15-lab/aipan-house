**Quick answer:** The Haridwar Kumbh 2027 runs from **Thursday 14 January to Tuesday 20 April 2027**. The official website lists **eleven bathing days** at Har Ki Pauri. Four are **Amrit Snans**, the ceremonial baths of the akharas: **6 March (Mahashivratri), 8 March (Phalguna Amavasya), 15 March (Dev Doli) and 14 April (Mesh Sankranti)**. The last of these is the main one. These are the dates currently published on the [official Snan and Events page](https://haridwarkumbh.uk.gov.in/snan-and-events), checked directly on 17 November 2026. Recheck the schedule and local access instructions before travelling.

## What the Haridwar Kumbh is

The Kumbh is held by turn at four places: Haridwar, Prayagraj, Ujjain and Nashik-Trimbakeshwar. The story behind it is the *samudra manthan*, the churning of the ocean. As the gods carried away the pot (*kumbh*) of *amrit*, drops fell at these four places. At Haridwar, tradition places the spot at **Brahmakund** in Har Ki Pauri. The district administration's own history page says so, and Uttarakhand Tourism's Har Ki Pauri page repeats it.

**The astrological rule.** Each Kumbh site is tied to a position of Jupiter (Brihaspati) and the Sun.
- **Full Kumbh at Haridwar:** held when **the Sun enters Mesha (Aries) while Jupiter is in Kumbha (Aquarius)**, in mid-April. Drik Panchang states this rule, and the Haridwar district site links the Maha Kumbh to Jupiter entering Aquarius.
- **Ardh (half) Kumbh at Haridwar:** Drik Panchang lists it under the Sun entering Mesha while Jupiter is in Simha (Leo). In practice it is held **about six years after the full Kumbh**.

For the 2027 event, the official schedule explicitly labels **14 April, Mesha Sankranti**, as the **Pramukh Amrit Snan**. That designation comes from the event schedule, rather than an independent calculation here.

Jupiter takes about 11.86 years to circle the zodiac, not 12, so the cycle occasionally slips by a year. The last full Kumbh at Haridwar came in **2021**, eleven years after 2010. Its notified mela period was shortened to **1–30 April 2021** during the COVID-19 pandemic; that should not be read as meaning no Kumbh-related bathing took place before April. The previous Ardh Kumbh at Haridwar was in **2016**.

## Ardh Kumbh or Kumbh? How 2027 is described

You will see both names, and both are used by official voices:

- **"Ardh Kumbh"**: used in the mela administration's April 2026 announcement as reported by PTI, in the Chief Minister's November 2025 statements ("Haridwar Ardh Kumbh 2027 will be celebrated as grandly as Purna Kumbh"), and by most news reports. It also fits the six-year cycle after 2021.
- **"Haridwar Kumbh 2027"**: used by the state government's releases and the official website, logo and song ("Kumbh Ka Parv Aa Gaya"), launched on 12 October 2026.

The proposed **format** also matters. Reporting of the November 2025 announcement describes plans for Purna Kumbh-style Amrit Snan processions and a four-month gathering.

**Our wording on this site:** we use **Haridwar Kumbh 2027**, matching the official website. We explain that the April 2026 administration announcement called the gathering **Ardh Kumbh**, six years after the 2021 full Kumbh. Those two names appear in different official and news contexts. This article does not predict the next full Kumbh’s dates.

## Har Ki Pauri and the ghats

**Har Ki Pauri** ("the steps of Hari") is the main bathing ghat and the focus of every Amrit Snan.
- **Brahmakund**, its inner stretch, is where tradition says the amrit fell, and where the evening Ganga Aarti is held.
- The district administration notes the belief that Vishnu's footprint is set in a stone in the upper wall of Har Ki Pauri, where the Ganga always touches it.
- Uttarakhand Tourism records the legend that King Vikramaditya built the ghat in memory of his brother Bharthari.
- The **evening Ganga Aarti** is a major reason visitors come to Har Ki Pauri. Check the day’s arrangements locally; an ordinary-day aarti visit and access during a major Kumbh bathing event can be very different.

**Do not assume unrestricted public access during an Amrit Snan.** Akhara processions and crowd controls can change when and where ordinary pilgrims may bathe. Restrictions reported during the 2021 Kumbh are historical examples, not the 2027 timetable. Use the current police and mela instructions to choose an open ghat.

Other places pilgrims visit around Haridwar, allowing separate travel time:
- **Maya Devi temple**, within walking distance.
- **Mansa Devi** on Bilwa Parvat, reached by cable car or on foot.
- **Chandi Devi** on Neel Parvat.
- **Daksh Mahadev** in Kankhal.

The official website has an [interactive mela map](https://haridwarkumbh.uk.gov.in/map-and-traffic-updates). Check it for current locations and advisories, and confirm arrangements on arrival. A proposed facility on a planning map should not be treated as already operational.

## The akharas and the Amrit Snan

The **Akhil Bharatiya Akhara Parishad** recognises **13 akharas**, the monastic orders whose processions lead the Amrit Snan. Public bathing arrangements depend on the ghat-access plan; do not assume a universal “after the akharas” slot.

| Tradition | Akharas |
|---|---|
| Shaiva (Sannyasi), 7 | Juna, Niranjani, Mahanirvani, Atal, Avahan, Anand, Agni |
| Vaishnava (Bairagi), 3 | Nirmohi Ani, Digambar Ani, Nirvani Ani |
| Udasin, 2 | Bada Udasin, Naya Udasin |
| Nirmal, 1 | Nirmal Panchayati (based in Kankhal, Haridwar) |

All 13 were named as agreeing to the 2027 arrangements at the November 2025 meeting. The **order and timing** of the processions is set by the mela administration with the akharas. Check the current instructions rather than relying on a previous Kumbh’s procession schedule. Give processions space and follow police directions on the route.

## Dev Doli and the connection with the hills

For people from Garhwal and Kumaon, the Haridwar Kumbh is not only a gathering of sadhus. It is also the time **the hill deities come down to the Ganga**.

A **dev doli** is the palanquin in which a village deity is carried in procession, to the beat of the **dhol and damau**. The same tradition lies behind the dola of our [Nanda Devi Mela](/festivals/nanda-devi-mela).

- In **March 2026**, a meeting chaired by the Mela Officer discussed the dolis' bath, route and halts. The report discussed an expectation of **500 to 700 dev dolis** from Garhwal and Kumaon. This was a planning estimate, not confirmed attendance. *(ETV Bharat; report cited below)*
- The organising committee's programme puts the gathering of the dolis at **Rishikesh on 14 March** and their **bath at Brahmakund on 15 March 2027**. *(Dainik Bhaskar; committee meeting report, 30 Aug 2026)* The official website directly confirms **15 March as Dev Doli (Amrit Snan)**. The preceding Rishikesh gathering is a reported committee plan; check its final route and timing with the organisers.
- In 2021 the dolis went in procession to Har Ki Pauri and were bathed at Brahmakund, but on a small, symbolic scale.

**The bathing season also connects with familiar hill traditions:**
- **14–15 January**: Makar Sankranti is [Ghughutiya / Uttarayani](/festivals/ghughutiya) in Kumaon.
- **Basant Panchami**: this season connects with Saraswati worship and the [Saraswati chowki](/aipan/saraswati-chowki-lotus). Family practices and the start of local Holi gatherings vary.
- **Mid-March**: [Phool Dei](/festivals/phool-dei) brings flowers to thresholds in the hills. It belongs to the same spring season; this does not establish an identical date for every local observance.
- **March**: [Kumaoni Holi](/festivals/kumaoni-holi) provides another connection with the region’s spring celebrations.
- **14 April**: the main Amrit Snan, Mesh Sankranti, is **[Bikhauti](/festivals/bikhauti)**, the spring solar transition associated with ritual bathing in Kumaon. Check the Syalde fair’s own programme separately.

See the full [Kumaoni festival calendar](/festivals/kumaoni-calendar).

## How to reach Haridwar

- **By train:** **Haridwar Junction (HW)** is the main station. The official website also lists Jwalapur, Motichur, Raiwala, Laksar, Roorkee and the two Rishikesh stations. Check Indian Railways for your actual service and any special trains announced for your dates. Allow extra time for station crowd controls.
- **By air:** **Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun** is about **37 km** away, per the Haridwar district administration. Travellers arriving through Delhi can continue by train or road; allow time for the onward journey.
- **By road:** Haridwar is about **220 km** from Delhi. Uttarakhand Roadways and private buses run from Delhi and across the state. From the Kumaon hills, the usual road is down to Haldwani or Kathgodam, then across the plains.
- **On snan days:** in 2021 outside vehicles were sent to outer parking by direction of arrival, and shuttle buses ran from there. These are historical arrangements, not a confirmed 2027 plan. Allow for diversions and walking, and use the current official travel instructions.

## Where to stay

The official site has a [Stay and Accommodation page](https://haridwarkumbh.uk.gov.in/services/stay-and-accommodation). Check its current information before booking. Options to compare include:

- **Hotels and guesthouses** in Haridwar (Har Ki Pauri, Upper Road, Jwalapur, Kankhal). Rooms near the ghats book out first for Amrit Snan dates.
- **Ashrams and dharamshalas.** Many take pilgrims, often with simple rooms and fixed gate times. Book directly, and ask about their rules on snan days.
- **State tourism accommodation** run by GMVN (Garhwal Mandal Vikas Nigam). Book through its official website.
- **Rishikesh** (about 25 km away). Consider it only after checking the final Dev Doli programme and transport arrangements. Road closures can make the trip slow on snan days.

**Prefer flexible bookings where practical.** The official schedule is published, but transport and access arrangements may change. Use the government website for official mela instructions. Hotels and legitimate booking providers can use other domains: verify the property, contact details, cancellation terms and payment recipient independently.

## Crowd and safety tips

1. **Pick your snan day first.** Amrit Snan days (6, 8, 15 March; 14 April) have the tightest road and ghat controls. An ordinary weekday outside the major bathing dates may be easier to manage, but a quiet visit is not guaranteed.
2. **Choose a ghat that is open to the public.** Arriving early does not override a closure. Follow current access instructions and avoid entering a closed procession area.
3. **Fix a meeting point** with your group before you enter the crowd, and note the nearest lost-and-found and police post on the official map.
4. **Hold on to children and elders.** Put a slip with a phone number in their pockets. Do not rely on a phone app alone: batteries, reception and crowd movement can make contact difficult.
5. **Respect the current.** Use only bathing areas open and designated for public use. Follow staff instructions, stay within barriers and do not treat a chain or railing as a guarantee of safety.
6. **Dress for the season.** Winter mornings can be cold and April afternoons hot. Check the forecast, carry suitable layers, bring drinking water and take breaks from the sun.
7. **Follow police and mela directions** on routes, one-way systems and barricades, especially near the akhara procession route.

## FAQ

### When is the Haridwar Kumbh 2027?
From 14 January to 20 April 2027, with eleven bathing days on the official website. These are the dates on the official Snan and Events page, checked directly on 17 November 2026. Recheck before travelling.

### How many Amrit Snans are there in 2027?
Four on the official website: 6 March, 8 March, 15 March (Dev Doli) and 14 April. The earlier government announcements named three (6 March, 8 March, 14 April).

### Which is the main Amrit Snan?
14 April 2027, Mesh Sankranti. The official website calls it the Pramukh (main) Amrit Snan.

### Is 2027 an Ardh Kumbh or a Purna Kumbh?
The April 2026 administration announcement, reported by PTI, calls it Ardh Kumbh. The current government website brands it “Haridwar Kumbh 2027”. Use that official event name, while recognising the Ardh Kumbh description in earlier coverage.

### What is Dev Doli?
The bath of Uttarakhand's village deities, carried down in their palanquins (dolis). The official website lists it on 15 March 2027. A committee programme reported in the press proposes a Rishikesh gathering on 14 March; confirm those arrangements separately.

### When was the last Haridwar Kumbh?
The last full Kumbh was in 2021, with a shortened notified mela period of 1–30 April during the pandemic. The last Ardh Kumbh was in 2016.
