**Harela** — literally "day of green" — is among the most loved festivals of Kumaon. It marks the onset of the monsoon sowing season and, in the Kumaoni reckoning, a turning of the year toward growth and abundance.

## The seven grains

Ten days before the festival, families sow seven kinds of grain in small baskets of soil indoors. Kept in low light and watered daily, the seedlings grow pale gold and tall. On Harela day they are cut — the *harela* — and placed on the heads and behind the ears of family members as a blessing.

## Rituals and foods

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sowing | Seven grains, ten days prior |
| Blessing | Cut seedlings placed on the head |
| Foods | Seasonal Kumaoni dishes, sweets |
| Deities | Especially associated with Shiva and Parvati |

## Why it matters

Harela ties the Kumaoni household to its agricultural calendar — a small green rite that says the year has turned and the fields are ready.
