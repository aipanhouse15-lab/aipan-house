Aipan is the ritual folk art of the Kumaon region of Uttarakhand: fine white lines drawn in rice-flour paste over a ground of red *geru* (ochre clay). For centuries it has marked the thresholds, prayer rooms and ceremonial seats of Kumaoni homes — never decoration for its own sake, but a language of auspiciousness.

<Padma />

## A language, not a decoration

Every Aipan pattern carries meaning. The art is traditionally drawn freehand by the women of the household, the rice paste applied with the fingertips or a thin twig, the lines kept deliberately unbroken — an unbroken line is an unbroken blessing.

## The red and the white

The contrast is the signature. The deep red of geru stands for the earth and the auspicious; the white of rice paste stands for purity and prosperity. Together they make Aipan instantly recognisable, even abstracted onto cloth or paper today.

## Where it appears

- At the **dehli** (threshold), to welcome and to guard
- Around the **chowki**, the low wooden seat used in rituals
- In the **puja** room, framing the space of worship
- On festival days, when each occasion calls for its own pattern
