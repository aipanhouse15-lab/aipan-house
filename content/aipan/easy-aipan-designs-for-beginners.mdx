You do not need to begin with a large floor design. Start with a short line of dots, learn to keep your pressure even, and add one shape at a time. These 15 ideas move from simple practice marks toward familiar Aipan forms such as the lotus, chowki, Swastika and Lakshmi Pad.

Some designs below are simplified exercises for learning, not separate traditional ritual patterns. Aipan forms and their use can vary between families and places. For the traditional materials and drawing technique, see [our step-by-step guide to making Aipan](/aipan/how-to-make-aipan); for the meaning and occasions of named motifs, see [the Aipan ritual guide](/aipan/aipan-ritual-significance).

## Start with Lines and Dots

These first designs help you practice spacing and steady pressure. Use paper first if you are new to rice paste; move to a geru-coated surface when your lines feel more controlled.

<Photo src="/images/aipan-dot-grid.jpg" alt="A hand marking a symmetrical grid of white dots on a red geru ground" caption="Evenly spaced bindu dots make it easier to keep a design balanced." />

### 1. A single dot-chain border

1. Draw two light guide lines about a finger-width apart.
2. Place rice-paste dots along the middle, keeping their size and spacing consistent.
3. Remove or cover the guides once the paste is dry.

### 2. A double dot-chain border

1. Make two parallel rows of evenly spaced dots.
2. Keep the gaps in one row aligned with the dots in the other.
3. Use this as a practice border around a small square or card.

### 3. A corner dot cluster

1. Mark the four corners of a square.
2. Add a small group of three or five dots at each corner.
3. Join the corners with a plain line or leave them separate for a lighter frame.

### 4. A straight-line frame

1. Draw a small square using four slow, continuous strokes.
2. Add a second square just inside or outside the first.
3. Practice keeping the gap between the two lines even all the way around.

## Try a Lotus and Chowki

Once dots and borders feel comfortable, practice one central motif at a time. The lotus and square frame are common building blocks in named Aipan compositions; the simplified versions here are for practice.

<Photo src="/images/saraswati-lotus-closeup.jpg" alt="A close view of a lotus motif at the centre of a Saraswati chowki" caption="Begin with four anchor petals, then add the diagonal petals between them." />

### 5. A four-petal lotus

1. Mark a centre dot and four anchor points: top, bottom, left and right.
2. Draw a rounded petal from each point back toward the centre.
3. Add a small ring or dot in the middle.

### 6. An eight-petal lotus

1. Draw the four cardinal petals first.
2. Add four more between them, at the diagonal directions.
3. Keep the petals rounded and similar in size; use the centre dot to check symmetry.

### 7. A lotus with a dotted centre ring

1. Draw a small eight-petal lotus.
2. Place evenly spaced dots in a circle just outside the petals.
3. Leave a little breathing room between the dots and petals so they do not merge.

### 8. A simple square chowki

1. Draw a small square frame.
2. Add a second inner square, then put one dot or short line at the centre.
3. Add a dot-chain outside the frame after the centre is complete.

### 9. A lotus inside a chowki

1. Draw a square frame and mark its centre with two crossing guide lines.
2. Draw the lotus in the middle, using the four main directions before filling the diagonals.
3. Add a simple border around the square. For the full ritual form, see [the Saraswati Chowki guide](/aipan/saraswati-chowki-lotus).

### 10. A chowki with four corner flowers

1. Make a small square with a central dot or lotus.
2. Draw one tiny four-petal flower just inside each corner.
3. Add a plain outer border so the corners remain easy to read.

## Add a Symbol or Threshold Design

The next designs use familiar symbols. If you are drawing for a ritual, follow the form and placement used in your own household rather than treating these practice steps as a substitute for family instruction.

<Photo src="/images/aipan-swastika-wall.jpg" alt="A white Aipan Swastika with lotus details on a red ochre wall" caption="The Swastika is one of the familiar auspicious symbols used in Kumaoni Aipan." />

### 11. A simple Swastika

1. Draw a short horizontal line, then a vertical line crossing its centre.
2. Add four short arms, turning them in the same clockwise direction.
3. Practice on paper first; keep the arms similar in length and the turns square.

### 12. A Swastika in a square

1. Draw a small square and mark its centre.
2. Draw a compact clockwise Swastika inside, leaving space between it and the frame.
3. Add four corner dots only if the square still feels uncluttered.

### 13. A paired Lakshmi Pad

1. Mark two heel positions on either side of an imaginary centre line.
2. Draw each footprint facing inward, with a rounded heel and simple toe marks.
3. Check that both footprints are similar in size and point toward the home. See [the Lakshmi Pad guide](/aipan/lakshmi-pad) for the traditional form and placement.

### 14. Lakshmi Pad with a lotus border

1. Draw the paired footprints first.
2. Add small rounded petals around the feet, keeping the walking path clear.
3. Finish with a dot-chain border. Keep this practice variation separate from any family-specific ritual layout.

### 15. A threshold design with footprints and diya

1. Draw a short, clear path of small footprints leading inward from the edge of your practice area.
2. Add a diya or flower on either side, leaving the centre open.
3. Use a narrow border to frame the arrangement. For the Lakshmi Pad used at a doorway, follow the detailed [motif guide](/aipan/lakshmi-pad).

<Photo src="/images/lakshmi-pad-threshold-diya.jpg" alt="Lakshmi Pad footprints and a lit diya at a decorated doorway threshold" caption="A threshold composition should leave a clear inward path for the footprints." />

## Choose What to Practice Next

Try the dot-chain border first, then a four-petal lotus, then a small chowki. When those feel steady, add a Swastika or practice the paired Lakshmi Pad on paper. The goal is even spacing and clean lines—not making the most elaborate pattern on the first try.

For materials, surface preparation and rice-paste consistency, continue with [How to Make Aipan](/aipan/how-to-make-aipan). For the named motifs, see the [Saraswati Chowki](/aipan/saraswati-chowki-lotus) and [Lakshmi Pad](/aipan/lakshmi-pad) guides.

## FAQ

### What is the easiest Aipan design for a beginner?

A short dot-chain border is a good starting exercise. It teaches even spacing and gentle, consistent pressure before you try curved petals or a larger composition.

### Which materials do I need for beginner Aipan?

The traditional base is red geru with white rice paste (bisvar). You can practice the shapes on paper first; the full materials and preparation steps are in [our Aipan how-to guide](/aipan/how-to-make-aipan).

### Are all 15 designs traditional Kumaoni patterns?

No. This list combines practice exercises and simplified variations with familiar named motifs such as the lotus, chowki, Swastika and Lakshmi Pad. Ritual forms and their placement vary; follow your family or local practitioner for ceremonial use.

### Can I draw Aipan on paper?

Yes. Thick handmade or watercolour paper is useful for practicing or making a portable piece. For a ritual floor or pata, use the surface and materials customary in your household.
