New to Aipan? Begin small. A neat row of dots teaches spacing; a simple square teaches control; petals become easier once you have a steady hand. This guide builds from those basics to a small chowki and familiar threshold motifs.

The numbered diagrams are practice sketches, not templates for every ritual. Aipan designs and their use can differ between Kumaoni families and places. If you are making Aipan for a ceremony, follow the form taught in your household or by a local practitioner. For materials and paste preparation, start with [our guide to making Aipan](/aipan/how-to-make-aipan); for context on named motifs, read [Aipan and its ritual significance](/aipan/aipan-ritual-significance).

## Before You Begin

Gather a small bowl of rice paste, a little water, a soft cloth and a piece of practice paper. For traditional floor Aipan, the surface is commonly prepared with red geru and the white design is drawn with rice paste. On paper, use a washable white paint if rice paste is too runny. Do not use a household floor or wall until you have permission and know how the surface should be prepared.

Keep the paste smooth enough to flow from your fingertips, but not so watery that it runs. Use your fingers or the drawing method customary in your home. First make five dots and five straight lines; move on when you can keep them similar in size. The [materials and technique guide](/aipan/how-to-make-aipan) has more detail.

## Dots and Borders

These first four exercises are about spacing. The diagrams show the idea; make each one small on paper before drawing it on a larger surface.

<img src="/images/easy-aipan-practice-dots.svg" alt="Four beginner Aipan practice diagrams: a dot-chain border, double dot rows, corner dot clusters and a double-line square frame" />

### 1. Single dot-chain

1. Draw a faint guide line with pencil, or imagine a straight path.
2. Place small dots along it, leaving about one dot-width between each.
3. Keep the dots similar in size; wipe away a pencil guide after practice.

### 2. Double dot-chain

1. Make two parallel rows of dots.
2. Keep the rows the same length and the gap between them even.
3. Try placing the dots in the second row opposite the gaps in the first.

### 3. Corner dot clusters

1. Draw a small square frame.
2. Put three or five dots just inside each corner.
3. Leave the middle of each side open, or connect the corners with a plain line.

### 4. Double-line frame

1. Draw a small square with four slow strokes.
2. Add a second square inside it, keeping the gap as even as you can.
3. Add corner dots only after the frame looks balanced.

## Lotus and Chowki

The next six ideas combine a centre, petals and a square frame. Start with light guide marks; they are there to help you find the centre, not to become part of the finished design.

<img src="/images/easy-aipan-practice-lotus-chowki.svg" alt="Six Aipan practice diagrams showing a four-petal lotus, eight-petal lotus, dotted lotus, square chowki and two framed lotus variations" />

### 5. Four-petal lotus

1. Place a centre dot and imagine a cross through it.
2. Draw one rounded petal toward the top, bottom, left and right.
3. Bring each petal back to the centre and keep the four shapes similar.

### 6. Eight-petal lotus

1. Draw the four main petals from the previous exercise.
2. Add four smaller petals in the diagonal gaps.
3. Check that the space around the centre is even before adding detail.

### 7. Lotus with a dot ring

1. Draw a small eight-petal lotus.
2. Place a ring of evenly spaced dots around it.
3. Leave a clear gap so the dots do not touch the petals.

### 8. Plain square chowki

1. Draw one square, then a smaller square inside it.
2. Put a dot in the centre of the inner square.
3. Add a dot row outside the frame if you want to practice a border.

### 9. Lotus in a square

1. Draw a square frame and mark its centre lightly.
2. Make a four-petal lotus around the centre, then add diagonal petals if there is room.
3. Leave space between the lotus and the frame. See the [Saraswati Chowki and lotus guide](/aipan/saraswati-chowki-lotus) for a fuller named composition.

### 10. Four-corner flower frame

1. Draw a small square with a dot or simple lotus in the centre.
2. Add a tiny four-petal flower inside each corner.
3. Keep the corner flowers smaller than the centre so the design stays easy to read.

## Symbols and Thresholds

These last five exercises use familiar motifs. The simple versions here are for hand practice; they do not replace the customary shape, direction or placement used for a household ritual.

<img src="/images/easy-aipan-practice-symbols.svg" alt="Five Aipan practice diagrams showing a simple Swastika, a framed Swastika, paired Lakshmi Pad, footprints with a lotus border, and a threshold arrangement" />

### 11. Simple Swastika

1. Draw a short upright line and a crossing horizontal line.
2. Extend each end into a short right-angle arm, turning all four the same way.
3. Practice on paper until the arms are even. For ceremonial use, follow your family’s form.

### 12. Swastika in a square

1. Draw a small square and mark the centre.
2. Draw a compact Swastika, leaving space between it and the frame.
3. Add one dot near each corner only if the frame still feels uncluttered.

### 13. Paired Lakshmi Pad practice

1. Mark two heel positions on either side of an imaginary centre line.
2. Draw two simple feet facing inward, with a rounded heel and small toe marks.
3. Check that both are similar in size. The [Lakshmi Pad guide](/aipan/lakshmi-pad) explains the motif and its customary placement.

### 14. Lakshmi Pad with a lotus border

1. Draw the paired footprints first, leaving a clear path between them.
2. Add small petals outside the path, not over the footprints.
3. Finish with a sparse dot border. Treat this as a practice arrangement, not a prescribed ritual layout.

### 15. Simple threshold arrangement

1. Draw a short inward-facing path of footprints.
2. Add one small flower or diya on either side, keeping the centre clear.
3. Frame the design with a narrow line. For traditional doorway placement, see the [Lakshmi Pad guide](/aipan/lakshmi-pad).

## What to Practice Next

Try designs 1, 4 and 5 first: a dot-chain, a square frame and a four-petal lotus. When those feel steady, put the lotus inside the square. There is no need to make a large or elaborate pattern at the beginning; even spacing and calm, continuous strokes matter more.

For paste consistency and surface preparation, continue with [How to Make Aipan](/aipan/how-to-make-aipan). Then explore the [Saraswati Chowki](/aipan/saraswati-chowki-lotus) and [Lakshmi Pad](/aipan/lakshmi-pad) guides for more context on those named forms.

## FAQ

### What is the easiest Aipan design for a beginner?

A short dot-chain is a good first exercise. It helps you practice spacing and steady pressure before you try curved petals or a larger composition.

### What do I need to practice Aipan?

Start with paper, a pencil for faint guides and a washable white medium. For traditional floor Aipan, households commonly use a red geru base and white rice paste; preparation can vary. See [our materials and method guide](/aipan/how-to-make-aipan).

### Are these 15 designs all traditional Aipan patterns?

No. The list includes basic drawing exercises and simplified practice arrangements alongside familiar motifs such as the lotus, chowki, Swastika and Lakshmi Pad. Ritual forms and their use vary, so follow your family or a local practitioner for ceremonial Aipan.

### Can I practice Aipan on paper?

Yes. Paper is a useful place to practice shapes before working on a prepared floor, wall or pata. Use a washable medium and follow the surface preparation customary in your household for ritual work.
